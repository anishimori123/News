# Teruaki Sato (佐藤 輝明)

> Left-handed power hitter from Nishinomiya, the city where Hanshin's Koshien Stadium stands. Unknown in high school, a record-breaker at Kindai University, the first pick of four clubs in the 2020 draft. In six seasons with the Hanshin Tigers he went from a rookie with 173 strikeouts, to being sent down to the farm team twice, to league MVP in 2025 and the Triple Crown (best batting average, most home runs, most runs batted in) in 2026. He left through the posting system (the rule that lets a Japanese club release a player to MLB in exchange for a fee) and got the biggest contract ever reported for a Japanese position player.

## 🧭 1. Character: quiet face, loud bat
Short answer: he is not a loud leader. Reports describe a calm, poker-faced man who does not show his feelings, and for years Hanshin fans read that as a lack of fight. What the record shows is a self-driven worker who went to America on his own to fix his swing, took two years of public scolding from his manager without answering back, and came out the other side as the best hitter in Japan.

### 😐 1. A quiet face that fans misread
He does not shout or show regret on the field. After his 2024 demotion, fans complained that he looked as if he did not care. A former Hanshin player answered that he was never that type and should not fake it; he just had to produce.

- Daily Sports column listing the fan complaints (chasing bad pitches, no visible regret, 'no fighting spirit') and former player Yoshihiro Nakata's reply. The columnist reads the anger as the other side of very high hopes. ([source](https://www.daily.co.jp/opinion-d/2024/05/16/0017657521.shtml))
- Number Web: coaches called him more sensitive than he looks. ([source](https://number.bunshun.jp/articles/-/866569))

### 🧱 2. He absorbed a hard manager
Manager Akinobu Okada (2023 to 2024) criticised him in the newspapers and sent him to the farm team more than other regulars. Okada later said this was about basics, not dislike, and that he rated the talent highly.

- Okada denied acting on likes and dislikes and said he wanted Sato to master basic plays. His words: 'Sato truly has ability.' ([source](https://number.bunshun.jp/articles/-/866569))
- Benched on 24 June 2023 and sent down over attitude and conduct; he said then that he would take the lesson to heart (reported second-hand a year later). ([source](https://www.daily.co.jp/opinion-d/2024/05/16/0017657521.shtml))
- After the 14 May 2024 error Okada said that was the end of it; Sato went to the farm team for about three weeks. ([source](https://www.daily.co.jp/tigers/2024/12/24/0018477441.shtml))

### 🛠️ 3. He went looking for answers himself
After winning the 2023 title he cut the team's celebration trip short and flew to Driveline Baseball in Seattle, a training lab that measures a swing with cameras and sensors. Two years later he trained with Dodgers star Mookie Betts on defence.

- Five days at Driveline in Dec 2023. The analysis showed his body drifting forward and a contact point too close to his body; he said he wanted to match feel and data. ([source](https://www.daily.co.jp/tigers/2024/01/15/0017227629.shtml))

### ⚖️ The honest view
- He strikes out a lot. 173 as a rookie, 163 in 2025, 327 over his last two seasons. MLB.com says he swung at 53% of pitches and chased 35.7% of pitches outside the strike zone, and called that 'playing with fire' against MLB pitching.
- CBS Sports puts his contact rate at 66% against an MLB average of 77%. A projection quoted by MLB.com (FanGraphs) expects .247 with 31 home runs in 2027: good power, modest average.
- His defence at third base was a real problem: 23 errors in 2024, the most in Japan. He won a Golden Glove a year later, but the Cubs plan to use him mainly in right field, where he has played 262 games.
- He was streaky for four years. Home runs went 24, 20, 24, 16 before jumping to 40. Only two of his six seasons are superstar seasons.
- The contract number is not official. $120 million comes from US reporters; another reporter says $125 million. The club announced the years, not the money.
- The price was helped by timing: few power hitters on the market, and Munetaka Murakami's 35 home runs for the White Sox in 2026 made clubs trust Japanese sluggers more. That is Chunichi Sports' reading, and several fans said the same.
- My observation: the page leans on Daily Sports, a paper that covers Hanshin every day and is friendly to the club. Where a claim rests on a headline that I could not open, it says so.

## 🔎 8. Deep dives

### 🚪 8.1 How the door to America opened (2024 to 2026)
Short version: in Japan a player needs nine years of service before he can leave freely. Before that, only his club can let him go, through the posting system. Hanshin had said no to others. Sato asked in 2024 after a bad year, was told to prove it, won the MVP, pushed again with an American agent, won the Triple Crown, and then the club said yes. The whole chase took 23 days once the window opened, because everyone wanted it done before the MLB lockout.

#### What this shows (my analysis)
- The pattern is new for Hanshin. The player hired a US agency early, the agency negotiated with the Japanese club directly, and the yearly salary talk became the lever: he stayed unsigned until the club at least agreed to keep talking about posting.

Two rules explain this table. In Japan a player signs a new one-year contract every winter, and the club raises or freezes his pay according to last season; he cannot move to another club for years, so he has little bargaining power. The posting system then lets his club release him to MLB early: all 30 MLB clubs can bid, which is the first time in his life that the open market sets his price. The Japanese club is paid a fee worked out from the contract.
