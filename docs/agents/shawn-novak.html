🌺 Shawn Novak ショーン・ノバック

The American agent behind Teruaki Sato's move from Hanshin to the Cubs. He is not a famous name in the USA; his niche is Japan. He can interpret Japanese at a press conference, he negotiated a foreign pitcher's contract WITH Hanshin in 2019 before he ever negotiated against them, and Japanese papers describe him as the man who demanded, in writing, that Hanshin let Sato go.

📌 Status (2026-11-27): Active. Player agent at VC Sports Group, reported to work from Hawaii. In November 2026 his client Teruaki Sato signed a 6-year contract with the Chicago Cubs, reported at $120 million. His client Tomoyuki Sugano is a free agent. He has given no interview that I could find.

📌 1. In short

Most Japanese stars pick a big American agency with a famous name. Sato picked a four-person firm and an agent almost unknown to US media. What that agent had was Japan: the language, a Hawaii base between the two countries, and a history with Hanshin from the other side of the table. The confirmed record is short, so this page separates three things: what solid sources say, what tabloids and magazines claim, and what I could not open.

🎯 4. Method: how he seems to work

Built from the cases above. The first three points are sourced; the rest is my analysis.
  • He speaks for the player in Japanese and English himself. At Sugano's Orioles introduction he was the interpreter, which a big agency would normally hire someone for. [source]
  • He negotiates with the Japanese club, not only the American one. Sato said his agent and Hanshin talked through posting and salary together. [source]
  • He sits between the two countries. Sponichi reported MLB scouts discussing trips to Hawaii to meet him. [source]
  • My analysis: he knew Hanshin's front office from the Suarez deal in 2019, when he was selling them a player. Six years later he was taking one away. That inside knowledge is probably his real edge, though no source says this is how Sato found him.
  • My analysis: he uses the yearly contract as leverage. Sato stayed unsigned until the day before camp and then signed for one year only, which kept the question open for October. [source]
  • My analysis: he is building a line of Hanshin clients. If Morishita is his too, the club will meet the same agent again in a few years.

💰 5. Who pays him

  • Not reported. In MLB the player normally pays the agent a percentage of the contract. No source gives his rate for Sato or Sugano.
  • Scale, for a sense of the stakes: if an agent's cut were a few percent of $120 million, it would be several million dollars. This is arithmetic on an unknown rate, not a reported figure.

⚠️ 7. Controversies and criticism

No misconduct, sanction or legal problem was found (searched 27 Nov 2026, Japanese and English; a light check). The criticism is about style.
  • Criticism: Kansai columnists and weeklies painted him as hard-line and as stirring up the club (see the rumour table). [source]
  • The defence: the result. His client got the posting within one season and a contract 50% above the market forecast, and thanked the club on the way out. Hanshin's head of baseball operations said the decision was the club's own overall judgement. [source]
  • Note on sources: almost everything negative comes from anonymous voices in outlets that lean toward the club's side. He has not answered in public.

🔭 9. Lessons (my analysis)

  • A small agent can beat big ones for a Japanese client if he covers the gap the big ones leave: the Japanese club, the language and daily contact.
  • The route in was a foreign player. Representing an import inside Japan (Suarez) gave him relationships with a Japanese front office, which later helped him sign Japanese stars. That is the reverse of the usual path.
  • The Japanese press treats an agent who talks to the club as a troublemaker. That says as much about how new this is in Japan as it does about him.
