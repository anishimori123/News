🏅 Sports

⚾ Hideo Nomo 野茂 英雄

Japanese pitcher with the twisting 'Tornado' windup. In January 1995, at 26, he 'retired' from the Kintetsu Buffaloes to get out of his Japanese contract, signed with the Los Angeles Dodgers and became the first Japanese player to move to Major League Baseball (MLB) for good. He was called a traitor at home, then won Rookie of the Year. The route was planned with agent Don Nomura. Every Japanese major leaguer since has walked through the door he reopened.

⚽ Maya Yoshida 吉田 麻也

Japanese centre-back (a defender who plays in the middle of the back line). He played in the top division of six countries, captained Japan at a World Cup, and captained LA Galaxy to the 2024 MLS Cup.

🤝 Tetsuro Kiyooka 清岡 哲朗

Japanese football agent who represented Keisuke Honda at his 2008 move to the Netherlands and is credited with Maya Yoshida's move there. Also an executive of the Japanese players' association during its 2010 pay fight with the Japan FA.

🇩🇪 Thomas Kroth トーマス・クロート

Former Bundesliga midfielder who became the German-side agent behind the first big wave of Japanese players in Germany: Takahara, Hasebe, Kagawa, Uchida and others. German media once called it a monopoly.

🚪 Hai Berden ハイ・ベルデン

Dutch logistics businessman who, as chairman of small club VVV-Venlo, gave Keisuke Honda, Maya Yoshida and others their first step into European football. A 'gateway' person: he is the reason the Dutch door existed.

🇳🇱 Kees Ploegsma Jr.

Dutch agent named in Keisuke Honda's 2009 sale from VVV-Venlo to CSKA Moscow, and quoted as Honda's agent through 2010. An example of a local agent taking over for the big sale.

⚾ Don Nomura 団 野村

The agent who got Hideo Nomo out of Japan in 1995 by finding a gap in the rules, then did it again with Hideki Irabu (1997) and Alfonso Soriano (1998). Those three fights forced Japanese and American baseball to create the posting system. Half American, half Japanese, stepson of the famous manager Katsuya Nomura, he was for years the most disliked man in Japanese baseball offices.

🏢 Wasserman (now THE·TEAM) ワッサーマン

A Los Angeles sports and entertainment agency founded in 2002. It became the main American agency for Japanese baseball stars (Darvish, Senga, Seiya Suzuki, Yamamoto, Sasaki) through one American agent, Joel Wolfe, building on the door first opened by Arn Tellem and Don Nomura. In 2026 the founder was forced out after his name appeared in the Epstein files, and the company changed its name.

🌉 Bridging Japanese athletes to the US 日本人選手とアメリカをつなぐ

A beginner's guide to how a Japanese baseball player gets to America, and who looks after daily life once he is there: language, food, housing, training, school and visas. It compares three routes (Japanese pro baseball then the 'posting' sale; a US college; a minor-league contract straight from high school), uses Rintaro Sasaki, Shotaro Morii and Shoki Oda as case studies, lists the companies already doing this as a business, and marks where support is thin for players who are not stars. Soccer (Maya Yoshida) is used only as a comparison.
