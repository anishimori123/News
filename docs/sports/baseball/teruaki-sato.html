Teruaki Sato 佐藤 輝明

Left-handed power hitter from Nishinomiya, the city where Hanshin's Koshien Stadium stands. Unknown in high school, a record-breaker at Kindai University, the first pick of four clubs in the 2020 draft. In six seasons with the Hanshin Tigers he went from a rookie with 173 strikeouts, to being sent down to the farm team twice, to league MVP in 2025 and the Triple Crown (best batting average, most home runs, most runs batted in) in 2026. He left through the posting system (the rule that lets a Japanese club release a player to MLB in exchange for a fee) and got the biggest contract ever reported for a Japanese position player.

📌 Status (2026-11-27): Signed with the Chicago Cubs (USA). The club announced a 6-year contract on 27 Nov 2026 (28 Nov in Japan). The reported total is $120 million, about 19.1 billion yen; one reporter says $125 million; the club has not published the money. His first press conference is on 30 Nov at Wrigley Field, Chicago. An MLB lockout (owners shutting the league during a labour dispute) is expected from 2 Dec.

🧭 1. Character: quiet face, loud bat

Short answer: he is not a loud leader. Reports describe a calm, poker-faced man who does not show his feelings, and for years Hanshin fans read that as a lack of fight. What the record shows is a self-driven worker who went to America on his own to fix his swing, took two years of public scolding from his manager without answering back, and came out the other side as the best hitter in Japan.

😐 1. A quiet face that fans misread

He does not shout or show regret on the field. After his 2024 demotion, fans complained that he looked as if he did not care. A former Hanshin player answered that he was never that type and should not fake it; he just had to produce.

  • Daily Sports column listing the fan complaints (chasing bad pitches, no visible regret, 'no fighting spirit') and former player Yoshihiro Nakata's reply. The columnist reads the anger as the other side of very high hopes. [source]
  • Number Web: coaches called him more sensitive than he looks. [source]

🧱 2. He absorbed a hard manager

Manager Akinobu Okada (2023 to 2024) criticised him in the newspapers and sent him to the farm team more than other regulars. Okada later said this was about basics, not dislike, and that he rated the talent highly.

  • Okada denied acting on likes and dislikes and said he wanted Sato to master basic plays. His words: 'Sato truly has ability.' [source]
  • Benched on 24 June 2023 and sent down over attitude and conduct; he said then that he would take the lesson to heart (reported second-hand a year later). [source]
  • After the 14 May 2024 error Okada said that was the end of it; Sato went to the farm team for about three weeks. [source]

🛠️ 3. He went looking for answers himself

After winning the 2023 title he cut the team's celebration trip short and flew to Driveline Baseball in Seattle, a training lab that measures a swing with cameras and sensors. Two years later he trained with Dodgers star Mookie Betts on defence.

  • Five days at Driveline in Dec 2023. The analysis showed his body drifting forward and a contact point too close to his body; he said he wanted to match feel and data. [source]
  • He studies video of Bryce Harper and Freddie Freeman, who also throw right and bat left. [source]
  • Five days of joint training with Mookie Betts in the USA in Dec 2025, on defence (headline and summary only; article not opened). [source]

🏆 4. Team first, even when asking to leave

When he first told Hanshin he wanted MLB, he said winning the title with Hanshin came before that. When the club said yes two years later, his first public words were thanks.

  • Dec 2024: 'This year was the first time I told them properly.' He also called his own 2024 season disappointing. [source]
  • Oct 2026: 'I am grateful to the club for respecting my wishes.' He said his days with his teammates were truly fun. [source]
  • Jan 2026, while unsigned: he said it was not a fight with the club and that they were talking properly. [source]

🏘️ 5. He gives back to his home town

He donates 100,000 yen to childhood cancer support for every home run, and runs baseball clinics for children in Nishinomiya.

  • 1.6 million yen donated after the 2024 season, 100,000 yen per home run (headline only; article not opened). [source]
  • Baseball clinic at Koto Elementary School in Dec 2025 and talk of a 'Sato-Teru Cup' youth tournament in Nishinomiya (headline only). [source]

🤝 6. Respected by a pitcher who faced him

Shota Imanaga, the Japanese pitcher who played for the Cubs, backed his move and said his power cannot be taught.

  • 14 Nov 2026 in Yokohama: Imanaga said the ability to hit the ball far 'is not something anyone can teach' and that Sato raises a team's strength. Their record: Sato 5-for-23 with one home run. [source]

⚖️ The honest view

  • He strikes out a lot. 173 as a rookie, 163 in 2025, 327 over his last two seasons. MLB.com says he swung at 53% of pitches and chased 35.7% of pitches outside the strike zone, and called that 'playing with fire' against MLB pitching.
  • CBS Sports puts his contact rate at 66% against an MLB average of 77%. A projection quoted by MLB.com (FanGraphs) expects .247 with 31 home runs in 2027: good power, modest average.
  • His defence at third base was a real problem: 23 errors in 2024, the most in Japan. He won a Golden Glove a year later, but the Cubs plan to use him mainly in right field, where he has played 262 games.
  • He was streaky for four years. Home runs went 24, 20, 24, 16 before jumping to 40. Only two of his six seasons are superstar seasons.
  • The contract number is not official. $120 million comes from US reporters; another reporter says $125 million. The club announced the years, not the money.
  • The price was helped by timing: few power hitters on the market, and Munetaka Murakami's 35 home runs for the White Sox in 2026 made clubs trust Japanese sluggers more. That is Chunichi Sports' reading, and several fans said the same.
  • My observation: the page leans on Daily Sports, a paper that covers Hanshin every day and is friendly to the club. Where a claim rests on a headline that I could not open, it says so.

🌱 6. Roots: where he comes from

🥋 A judo champion's son

His father, Hironobu Sato, was born in Miyagi in 1967, went to Sendai Ikuei High School and Nippon Sport Science University, and won the Kodokan Cup in 1991 in the 86 kg class. He was vice-captain of the university judo club when Olympic champion Toshihiko Koga was captain. He now teaches at Kwansei Gakuin University. [source]

🏋️ Weights at home

The father set up a bench press and dumbbells in the house. He taught punctuality, keeping promises, handing in schoolwork on time, and thinking only about what you can control. The son started lifting in his second year of high school and says that is when his power came. [source]

👴 A grandfather and a tennis court in Miyagi

His grandfather Isao taught him the basics of baseball at a tennis court near the grandparents' home. Isao died around 2024, shortly after watching one of his games. His grandmother Michie, 87, wrote in Daily Sports that when he points to the sky after a home run she thinks he is greeting his grandfather. [source]

🏟️ A Koshien kid

Nishinomiya is the home of Koshien Stadium. He grew up in the stands, was a member of the Hanshin fan club and admired Tomoaki Kanemoto and Kyuji Fujikawa. Fujikawa later became his manager in his two best seasons. [source]

🏫 Unknown in high school

Nigawa Gakuin is not a baseball power. He entered at 177 cm and 65 kg, and a Kobe Shimbun headline says he even thought about the soccer club. His team lost in the first round in his last summer. He chose university while keeping the dream of turning professional. [source]

🎓 Kindai University: the record

He batted cleanup from his first autumn. On 18 Oct 2020 he hit his 14th league home run, a three-run shot in the 11th inning, to break Tomohiro Nioka's Kansai Big6 record, with scouts from seven clubs watching. [source]

🎟️ Draft night: four clubs, one ticket

Orix, Hanshin, SoftBank and Yomiuri all picked him first on 26 Oct 2020. Manager Akihiro Yano drew the winning ticket with his right hand and said he drew on intuition. Sato called Hanshin a familiar local club. [source]

🎤 Off the field

He is a known fan of the idol group Momoiro Clover Z; in 2022 he publicly congratulated a member on her marriage and said he would keep supporting her (headline only). No marriage of his own has been reported. [source]

🔎 8. Deep dives

🚪 8.1 How the door to America opened (2024 to 2026)

Short version: in Japan a player needs nine years of service before he can leave freely. Before that, only his club can let him go, through the posting system. Hanshin had said no to others. Sato asked in 2024 after a bad year, was told to prove it, won the MVP, pushed again with an American agent, won the Triple Crown, and then the club said yes. The whole chase took 23 days once the window opened, because everyone wanted it done before the MLB lockout.

Why Hanshin said yes (the club's own words, through Satoshi Shimamura, head of baseball operations)

Why the Cubs, and why the gateway was open

  • A gap in the outfield: Seiya Suzuki and Ian Happ both became free agents, so the Cubs needed a corner outfielder with power. Alex Bregman already plays third base. [source]
  • They had done the homework: more than 50 scouting looks over two years, according to The Athletic as quoted by MLB.com. He also played against the Cubs in Tokyo on 14 March 2025. [source]
  • A human link: his Hanshin manager Kyuji Fujikawa pitched for the Cubs from 2013 to 2015. [source]
  • Earlier Japanese players went first: Suzuki (2022) and Imanaga (2024) both joined the Cubs from Japan. Both are free agents now, so he may arrive with no Japanese teammate. Whether either returns is not known. [source]
  • Other clubs linked in reports: Yankees, Mets, Dodgers, Phillies and Padres; the Padres' general manager scouted him in Japan in September. Which clubs made real offers is not confirmed. [source]
  • Tailwind (Chunichi Sports' reading): few big sluggers on the market, and Munetaka Murakami hit 35 home runs for the White Sox in 2026. [source]

The lockout clock

  • MLB's labour agreement between owners and players ends on 1 Dec 2026 at 11:59 p.m. US Eastern time. Owners are expected to lock the players out, which freezes all signings. [source]
  • Yahoo! News Japan's summary says this made 1 Dec the real deadline for his talks, even though the posting window formally runs longer. [source]

What this shows (my analysis)

  • The pattern is new for Hanshin. The player hired a US agency early, the agency negotiated with the Japanese club directly, and the yearly salary talk became the lever: he stayed unsigned until the club at least agreed to keep talking about posting.

Why his pay changed, year by year

Two rules explain this table. In Japan a player signs a new one-year contract every winter, and the club raises or freezes his pay according to last season; he cannot move to another club for years, so he has little bargaining power. The posting system then lets his club release him to MLB early: all 30 MLB clubs can bid, which is the first time in his life that the open market sets his price. The Japanese club is paid a fee worked out from the contract.
