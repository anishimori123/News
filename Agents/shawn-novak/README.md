## 📌 1. In short
Most Japanese stars pick a big American agency with a famous name. Sato picked a four-person firm and an agent almost unknown to US media. What that agent had was Japan: the language, a Hawaii base between the two countries, and a history with Hanshin from the other side of the table. The confirmed record is short, so this page separates three things: what solid sources say, what tabloids and magazines claim, and what I could not open.

- My analysis: he knew Hanshin's front office from the Suarez deal in 2019, when he was selling them a player. Six years later he was taking one away. That inside knowledge is probably his real edge, though no source says this is how Sato found him.
- My analysis: he uses the yearly contract as leverage. Sato stayed unsigned until the day before camp and then signed for one year only, which kept the question open for October. ([source](https://www.daily.co.jp/tigers/2026/01/31/0019970204.shtml))
- My analysis: he is building a line of Hanshin clients. If Morishita is his too, the club will meet the same agent again in a few years.

## 🔭 9. Lessons (my analysis)
- A small agent can beat big ones for a Japanese client if he covers the gap the big ones leave: the Japanese club, the language and daily contact.
- The route in was a foreign player. Representing an import inside Japan (Suarez) gave him relationships with a Japanese front office, which later helped him sign Japanese stars. That is the reverse of the usual path.
- The Japanese press treats an agent who talks to the club as a troublemaker. That says as much about how new this is in Japan as it does about him.
