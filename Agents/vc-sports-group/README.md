## 📌 1. In short
Most top Japanese pitchers in MLB use one agency, Wasserman. Sato chose a different one about two years before he left Japan. That agency talked to his Japanese club for him, which is still unusual in Japan, and finished his MLB deal in 23 days.

## 🎯 4. Method: what it did for Sato
- It negotiated with the Japanese club, not only with MLB clubs. Sato said his agent and Hanshin talked through posting and salary together. ([source](https://news.yahoo.co.jp/articles/6f3424a228fe40ccc674bc5bdff5766a875c13ba))
- It used the yearly contract as the moment to press. Sato stayed unsigned until 30 Jan 2026, then signed for one year only, with posting talks continuing. ([source](https://www.daily.co.jp/tigers/2026/01/31/0019970204.shtml))
- It moved fast once the window opened: posted 1 Nov, agreed 24 Nov, ahead of the 1 Dec labour deadline. ([source](https://www.mlb.com/news/teruaki-sato-mlb-posting-system))
- It connected him with a star client: he trained with Mookie Betts in Dec 2025 (headline only). ([source](https://www.daily.co.jp/tigers/2025/12/19/0019828890.shtml))
- The result beat the market forecast: MLB Trade Rumors had predicted 5 years and $80 million. ([source](https://www.mlbtraderumors.com/2026/11/cubs-to-sign-teruaki-sato.html))

## 🔭 7. Lessons (my analysis)
- A hitter does not have to use the agency the pitchers use. A record contract for a star hitter (Betts) was the selling point here.
- Hiring the agent two years early mattered more than the 23 days of bidding. The hard part was getting the Japanese club to say yes.
- If Morishita really has signed too, Hanshin now face the same agency for their next star. Worth watching.
